# Changelog

All notable changes to SOURSOP are documented in this file.

## 2.0.6 (unreleased)

A bug-fix release, focussed on absolute correctness including direct comparison to alternative implementations of some of the tools we re-implement here  (e.g. mdtraj/numpy from first principles, the Poulsen random-coil server's own JavaScript, HDXer, the Pesce et al. Rh scripts, brute-force reweighting solves). Introduces small bug fixes and defensive programming patterns that span across `ssprotein`, `sstrajectory`, `sshdx`, `ssnmr`, `sspre`, `ssbme`, `sscoper` and `sssampling` - plus docstring corrections and a regression test for every fix. 

One small new feature - `SSTrajectory` now checks on load that every chain is whole (not wrapped across the periodic boundary), see below - plus a few new keyword arguments (`seed`, `pseudocount`, `verbose` in `sssampling`; `hbond_method`, `hbond_cutoff`, `hbond_exclude_neighbours` in `sshdx`; `fit_kwargs` on `BME.scan_theta`; `check_whole_molecules` on `SSTrajectory`). Several fixes change numerical output; each is flagged **Numerical change** below.

### Bug fixes - ssprotein
* `get_hydrodynamic_radius(mode='kr')`: **Fixed the Kirkwood-Riseman prefactor. Numerical change.** The Kirkwood formula is `1/Rh = (1/N^2) sum_{i != j} <1/r_ij>`; the implementation returned `1 / <1/r_ij>` over the unordered pairs, i.e. it was missing the factor `N/(N-1)` and underestimated Rh by a fraction `1/N` (about 1% for a 100-residue chain, 17% for the six-residue GS6 test peptide). The prefactor now matches Kirkwood (1954), Nygaard et al. (2017) and the Pesce et al. (2022) reference implementation this mode is meant to reproduce. Also **fixed `R1`/`R2` being silently ignored**: the branch looped over every CA-bearing residue in the chain and never consulted the requested region, so a sub-region returned the whole-chain value. The sum now runs over the pairs of CA-bearing residues inside `[R1, R2]`; caps never contribute; a region with fewer than two CA-bearing residues raises an `SSException`.
* `get_hydrodynamic_radius(mode='nygaard')`: **Fixed the inputs to the Nygaard equation. Numerical change.** Nygaard et al. calibrated equation 7 on the $R_g$ of the C-alpha atoms and on `N`, the number of residues; the implementation fed it the all-atom Rg (hydrogens and sidechains included, 5-7% larger than the CA Rg on the bundled fixtures) and a cap-inclusive residue count, biasing Rh high by a few percent on all-atom chains (and by 18% on the capped six-residue test peptide, where `N` was 8 rather than 6). Both modes now work on the CA-bearing residues of the region: `N` counts them and the Rg is that of their CA atoms. The earlier 2.0.6 fix in which a sub-region used the whole-chain `N` is subsumed by this.
* `get_t`: **Fixed the chain length ignoring the region.** `N` was the whole-chain `n_residues` even when `R1`/`R2` selected a sub-region. It is now the number of residues in the (inclusive) region; the default call is unchanged.
* `get_secondary_structure_BBSEG(R1, R2)`: **Fixed an explicit region coming back two residues short.** The topology was sliced to `[R1, R2]` before phi/psi were computed, so phi(R1) (which needs the carbonyl C of R1-1) and psi(R2) (which needs the amide N of R2+1) were undefined and the two boundary residues were dropped. The dihedrals are now taken from the whole chain (memoised by `get_angles`) and then restricted to the region. Default calls are unchanged.
* `get_secondary_structure_DSSP(R1, R2)`: **Fixed a sub-region's assignment differing from the whole-chain one.** DSSP was run on the isolated region, which dropped H-bond partners outside it and changed the assignment even at interior residues (9 of 11 residues of one CTL9 region moved). DSSP (and the two-bead CA-trace detector) now run once over the whole CA-bearing chain and the requested columns are selected. A region that includes a cap now raises an `SSException`: caps have no backbone for DSSP, and mdtraj's DSSP intermittently segfaults the interpreter on cap-inclusive trajectories (reproduced 5 times out of 6 on the CTL9 fixture). Default calls are unchanged.
* `get_contact_map`: **Fixed a wrongly dimensioned map on chains with FOR or NH2 caps.** Caps were excluded by name (`ACE`/`NME` only), so a `FOR`/`NH2` cap was handed to mdtraj, which allocated a row for it and shifted every other row by one relative to the distance map. The atoms are now those of the CA-bearing residues, as in every other per-residue map. `mode='sidechain'`/`'sidechain-heavy'` on a one-bead coarse-grained chain raises instead of failing inside mdtraj.
* `get_all_SASA(mode='sidechain' | 'backbone' | 'all')`: **Fixed terminal groups being counted as sidechain. Numerical change for the terminal residues of uncapped chains.** mdtraj's `backbone` selector is N/CA/C/O only; the N-terminal amine hydrogens (H1/H2/H3) and the C-terminal carboxylate oxygens (OXT and its variants) therefore landed in the sidechain bucket - about 25% of the terminal residues' "sidechain" area on NTL9. They are now backbone. Also fixed the three arrays returned by `mode='all'` not being column-aligned on capped chains (the residue array spanned every residue, the other two only CA-bearing residues); all three are now `(n_frames, n_CA)`.
* `get_gyration_tensor` (and hence `get_asphericity`, `get_acylindricity`, `get_prolateness`): **moments are now taken about the geometric centre. Small numerical change** (relative differences below 1e-3 on all-atom chains, none on one-bead chains). The tensor was the unweighted second moment about the mass-weighted centre of mass, a mixed convention that left `trace(T)` above `Rg**2`; it now matches `get_radius_of_gyration` (mdtraj's `compute_rg`, which weights every atom equally) exactly.
* `get_distance_map`: **Fixed `RMS=True` returning the standard deviation of the squared distance (in Å²) as the std map. Numerical change for the `RMS=True` std map.** The std map is now the spread of the distance in Å whether or not `RMS` is set. Also fixed `return_instantaneous_maps=True` handing back squared distances when `RMS=True`.
* `get_local_collapse` and `get_local_heterogeneity`: **Fixed default histogram bins in nm.** Both defaults (`0-10` in steps of `0.01`) predate the switch to Ångström output, so every local Rg or intra-window RMSD of 10 Å or more silently fell outside the histogram (63% of the values for a 40-residue window on CTL9). Both defaults are now `np.arange(0, 100, 0.1)` Å. Only the default `histo`/`bins` outputs change.
* `get_contact_map`: **Fixed the contact map, alone among SOURSOP's distances, using minimum-image distances. Numerical change for chains that span more than half their box.** The `md.compute_contacts` call took mdtraj's default `periodic=True`; on the bundled GROMACS single-chain fixture (7.4 nm box, chain extent up to 7.1 nm) that turned residue pairs 50-70 A apart into 5-30 A "contacts" and put the terminal-pair contact fraction at a 45 A threshold at 0.90 instead of 0.55. Every distance in SOURSOP is now a plain Euclidean distance; see the whole-molecule contract below.
* `get_Q`: **Fixed the method overwriting the trajectory's coordinates.** The internal `superpose` ran on `self.traj` itself whenever `stride=1`, rotating every frame in place and corrupting anything positional computed afterwards (most visibly `SSTrajectory.get_interchain_distance`). Q depends only on pairwise distances and is unchanged beyond float32 round-off. `get_Q` also **raises when the reference frame has no native contacts** (a disordered reference, or a region too small to hold any) instead of returning an all-NaN array with a numpy warning, and validates `native_state_reference_frame`.
* `get_local_to_global_correlation`: **raises when fewer than two frames remain after striding** (the default `stride=20` on a trajectory of 20 frames or fewer) instead of silently returning an all-NaN correlation vector.
* `get_amino_acid_sequence(oneletter=True)`: **Fixed a bare `KeyError` on force-field residue names.** AMBER/CHARMM protonation and tautomer names (`HIE`/`HID`/`HIP`, `HSD`/`HSE`/`HSP`, `ASH`, `GLH`, `LYN`, `CYX`, `CYM`) and the `NMA`/`NAC` spellings of the NME cap are folded onto the parent residue via the new `ssdata.RESIDUE_NAME_ALIASES` / `ssdata.normalize_residue_name`; an unknown name raises an `SSException` naming it. This also took out the PENGUIN excluded-volume path on any such chain. The three-letter sequence keeps the force-field names.
* `get_residue_COM(resid, atom_name)`: an atom name that does not occur in the residue raises instead of silently returning `(0, 0, 0)` for every frame.
* Out-of-range residue indices now raise an `SSException` everywhere they used to slip through: `get_residue_atom_indices` / `get_all_atomic_indices` (returned an empty list and cached a bogus entry), `get_residue_mass` (returned 0), `get_inter_residue_atomic_distance` (mdtraj parser error), `get_site_accessibility(mode='resid')` and `get_regional_SASA` (`IndexError`, or `0` for `R1 >= R2`). `get_RMSD` validates `frame1` and `frame2` (a negative `frame1` silently counted from the end); `get_clusters` validates `stride` (`stride=0` was a `ZeroDivisionError`); a non-integer `stride` raises everywhere instead of being truncated on some paths and crashing with a `TypeError` on others. `get_angles` and `get_dihedral_mutual_information` raise on one-bead coarse-grained chains instead of returning empty arrays / a `0 x 0` matrix; `get_angle_decay` raises a clear error when an atom is missing (two-bead chains have no backbone C/N and failed inside numpy).
* `get_sidechain_alignment_angle`: the dot product is clipped to `[-1, 1]` before `arccos`, so (anti)parallel sidechains give 0 / 180 degrees rather than `NaN`; one-bead coarse-grained chains raise a clear `SSException`; the `sidechain_atom_1`/`sidechain_atom_2` overrides that the docstring promised (but which always raised) are implemented; and the error for a missing `R2` sidechain atom names the right residue.
* `get_secondary_structure_DSSP` **raises on one-bead coarse-grained chains** instead of silently returning 100% coil.
* `get_scaling_exponent(end_effect=0)`: fixed an `IndexError` from the `[a:-0]` slice being empty.
* `get_multiple_CA_index`: accepts NumPy integer scalars (previously `TypeError: not iterable`).
* `get_inter_residue_COM_distance` (and therefore `get_distance_map(mode='COM')`): `stride` is validated like every other strided method.
* `get_angle_decay(return_all_pairs=True)`: the pair dictionary is keyed by actual residue ids rather than 1-based positions in the CA-bearing list.

### Bug fixes - sstrajectory, sshdx, ssnmr, sspre
* `SSTrajectory`: **the whole-molecule input contract is now stated and checked.** SOURSOP has always computed every size, distance and contact directly from the coordinates with no periodic-boundary correction, so a chain that the simulation engine wrapped back into the box gave silently wrong results. The contract is now spelled out in the `SSTrajectory` docstring, `docs/modules/sstrajectory.rst` and `docs/usage/overview.rst`, and `SSTrajectory` tests for it on load: if in any frame two consecutive residues of a chain have CA atoms further apart than half the shortest box vector - the signature of a wrapped molecule; a whole chain larger than the box is not flagged - an `SSWarning` names the chain, the residue pair and the number of affected frames, with the `gmx trjconv` / mdtraj commands that fix it. `check_whole_molecules='raise'` turns that into an `SSException`, `False` skips the check, and the per-chain report is available at any time from the new `SSTrajectory.check_molecules_whole()`. Trajectories without a usable unit cell cannot be tested and are reported as such.
* `sshdx.compute_Nh`: **the H-bond count now follows Best & Vendruscolo. Numerical change for every default `ln P`.** The module cited Best-Vendruscolo and used their `beta_h = 2.0`, but counted backbone-to-backbone Wernet-Nilsson H-bonds at `|i - j| > 2`. Best & Vendruscolo (Structure 14:98) identify a hydrogen bond by "a cutoff of 2.4 Å between the donor hydrogen and the acceptor" with no sequence exclusion, and the Best lab's reference implementation (HDXer) counts every protein oxygen within 2.4 Å of the amide H accordingly, which finds several times more H-bonds (119 vs 21 over the ten NTL9 frames) and moves the ensemble-mean `ln P` by about 0.3 on the folded fixtures. The new default is `hbond_method='distance'` with `hbond_cutoff=0.24` nm; the 2.0.5 definition is kept as `hbond_method='wernet-nilsson'`, and `hbond_exclude_neighbours` imposes a sequence exclusion on either. `compute_protection_factors` forwards all three. `compute_Nh` on a hydrogen-free topology raises like `compute_Nc` (it returned an `(n_frames, 0)` array).
* `sshdx` and `ssnmr.compute_NOE_distances`: **distances are no longer minimum-imaged. Numerical change for pairs further apart than half the box.** Both used mdtraj's default `periodic=True`, unlike every other distance in SOURSOP; on a GROMACS chain that spans more than half its box the NOE distance for the terminal CA pair came back as 40 Å where the chain geometry gives 53 Å, and with `r^-6` averaging the spurious short distances dominate. Both now use `periodic=False`.
* `sshdx`: **Cap residues and the free N-terminal amine are no longer treated as exchangeable backbone amides.** `NME`/`NMA`/`NH2`-type caps and a first residue with no preceding backbone carbonyl were returning a ln P value. A residue preceded by `ACE` is a genuine amide and is retained.
* `sshdx` and `ssnmr` (`compute_J3_HN_HA`, `compute_NOE_distances`): `stride` is validated through the new `ssutils.validate_stride` (a `stride` of `0` raised a bare `ValueError`, a negative one reversed the trajectory).
* `ssnmr.compute_random_coil_chemical_shifts`: **Perdeuteration corrections now follow the live Poulsen random-coil server** (Maltsev, Ying & Bax, *J. Biomol. NMR* 2012); the tables in the port were the older Cavanagh set that the server has commented out, so `use_perdeuteration=True` disagreed with the web tool by 0.1-0.3 ppm on CA/CB. **Numerical change** for `use_perdeuteration=True` only; everything else in the port was executed against the server's JavaScript over 6006 sequence/temperature/pH cases and matched field for field. An unbalanced parenthesis in the sequence raises an `SSException`, and an unrecognised character (which is skipped, shortening the output and shifting the neighbour context of the residues around it) now emits an `SSWarning` naming it.
* `SSTrajectory.get_interchain_distance_map`: rows and columns are indexed by position among the CA-bearing residues rather than by `resid - 1 if ncap else resid`, which indexed past the end of the map on a chain with any other CA-less residue.
* `SSTrajectory.get_interchain_distance(periodic=True)`: returns an `np.ndarray` as documented rather than a Python list, which made `get_interchain_contact_map(mode='atom', periodic=True)` crash.
* `SSTrajectory(extra_valid_residue_names='XXX')`: a bare string is treated as a single residue name instead of being split into characters.
* `sspre.generate_PRE_profile`: on a one-bead coarse-grained chain the label-cloud model now falls back to the `CA` bead for both the anchor and the target (the docstring claimed CA-only chains worked, but the default `CB`/`N` atom names raised). A target atom that exists in no residue, `n_label_conformers < 1`, and a label cloud that is sterically excluded in every frame now raise instead of returning an all-NaN profile. The `__repr__` and the `tau_c` warning report the right quantity and units.
* `ssmutualinformation.calc_MI`: the docstring example used bins that did not cover its own data and raised; it now runs.

### Bug fixes - ssbme, sscoper, sssampling
* `COPER` / `iCOPER`: **Fixed feasible multi-group problems being reported infeasible.** Step 1 minimised the *summed* per-group chi-squared, which can push one group far below the limit while another sits above it even when a point satisfying every group exists (about 1% of random two-group problems on a small test case). When the summed minimiser fails a per-group check with more than one group, a second minimisation of the total squared violation `sum_a max(chi2_a - limit, 0)^2` (whose zero set is exactly the feasible region) now settles the verdict; `metadata['feasibility_check']` records which check did. Single-group problems are unchanged.
* `COPER` / `iCOPER`: **a prior with any zero weight no longer crashes.** Frames with zero prior weight are excluded from the optimisation and get weight exactly zero, with the remainder renormalised; `metadata['n_zero_prior_frames']` records how many (now also on the `iCOPER` result, which dropped it).
* `chi2_limit_scan`: the knee is chosen among the *feasible* limits (an infeasible limit has no maximum-entropy solution, so it must not be selected while a feasible one exists); infeasible points are excluded with an `SSWarning`, and a scan with no feasible limit warns.
* `theta_scan` / `BME.fit(auto_theta=True)` / `BMECustom.scan_theta`: a fit that fails carries `chi2 = nan`, which made the knee finder silently return the smallest theta whenever every fit failed. Failed fits are now excluded from the knee selection with an `SSWarning`, and a scan in which every fit failed raises. `BME.fit(auto_theta=True)` also forwards `max_iterations`/`optimizer` to the per-theta fits (they were silently dropped); `BME.scan_theta` gained `fit_kwargs`.
* `BME` / `iBME` / `BMECustom` / `iCOPER`: a prior with negative, non-finite or all-zero weights raises at construction (`BMECustom` used to report a *successful* fit built on a negative prior; `COPER` already rejected them).
* `iBME` / `iCOPER`: a single observable with `fit_offset=True` silently produced `scale=nan`; the underdetermined fit now raises. Exactly two observables with `fit_offset=True` is exactly determined (a scale and an offset map any two averages onto the two targets, so chi-squared is zero for every weight vector and the weights stay at the prior) and now emits an `SSWarning`.
* `BMEResult.diagnostics`: the high chi-squared warning compared the *reduced* chi-squared against `2 * n_observables`, so it almost never fired for more than one observable; the threshold is now a reduced chi-squared of 2.
* `BMEResult.predict` / `COPERResult.predict`: a 1D `(n_frames,)` input broadcast to `(n, n)` and came back unchanged rather than averaged.
* `sssampling.quality_plot`: **Fixed the dihedral panels being one residue to the left of the helicity panel on uncapped chains.** All four panels now share one residue axis, the 1-based position of each residue among the CA-bearing residues (caps not counted); on an uncapped chain the dihedral panels run from position 2 to `n - 1` and the helicity panel from 1 to `n`. Capped chains are unchanged.
* `sssampling`: **Fixed the excluded-volume reference dihedral lookup ignoring the residue itself.** Both keys into the `PHI`/`PSI` tables were derived from the neighbouring residue, so residue *i* was scored against the wrong distribution. **Numerical change** for every `SamplingQuality` built without a `reference_list` on a non-homopolymeric sequence.
* `sssampling`: `ref_pdfs('joint')` and `trj_pdfs('joint')` shared a cache key; the fractional-helicity panel ignored `proteinID`; one trajectory paired with several references crashed deep in the dihedral code (mismatched counts now raise up front); `quality_plot(dihedral='phi'|'psi')` under the 2D method crashed (unsupported combinations now raise); trajectories or references whose chains carry a different set of phi/psi residues raise an `SSException` instead of an inhomogeneous-array `ValueError`.

### Bug fixes - sssampling (dedicated audit)
* **Relative entropy was `inf` almost everywhere.** `compute_dihedral_rel_entropy` and `get_all_to_all_trj_comparisons(metric='relative entropy')` fed raw histograms to KL, which is infinite wherever the reference bin is empty and the trajectory bin is not - on the bundled fixtures that was 12/12 values, and 108/110 on the default excluded-volume path. Both methods now take `pseudocount=1e-6` (a per-bin mass added to both distributions before renormalising, via the new `smooth_pdf` helper); `pseudocount=0` restores the raw value. **Numerical change**: values that were `inf` are now finite.
* **`compute_pdf` normalised with the constructor's `bwidth` regardless of the `bins` passed**, so custom bin edges gave probability "masses" summing to 0.5 or 2. The per-bin width is now used. Default-grid output is unchanged.
* **The excluded-volume reference was resampled incorrectly.** `PrecomputedDihedralInterface.sample_angles` mapped uniform draws through `np.interp` over bin *centres*, which smears the distribution and never samples the ±180° tails (where phi peaks). It now does proper inverse-CDF sampling and reproduces the reference tables to Monte Carlo noise. **Numerical change** for every `SamplingQuality` built without a `reference_list`.
* The EV resampling used the unseeded global RNG, so two identical `SamplingQuality` objects scored differently. `SamplingQuality` and `PrecomputedDihedralInterface` take a `seed` argument (default `None` keeps the previous non-deterministic behaviour).
* **`bwidth` was silently rounded to whole degrees and the bin grid overshot 180°** (7.5° became 7° with edges to 184°; anything below 0.5° rounded to zero and crashed). `bwidth` must now divide 360° into a whole number of bins and the edges are built with `np.linspace` so they end exactly at 180°.
* **`quality_plot` crashed on any uncapped chain** because the helicity panel reused the per-dihedral x-axis. Panel D also shows the mean reference helicity when a `reference_list` was supplied, panel A's title reflects whether the comparison is to a reference ensemble or the EV limit, and the saved file is `<dihedral>_<figname>`. `compute_joint_hellinger_distance` is clamped at zero so identical distributions cannot return `NaN` from round-off.
* Trajectories (or trajectory/reference pairs) with different frame counts raise an `SSException` pointing at `truncate=True`; `top_file` / `ref_top` accept a per-trajectory list on every load path; unsupported selectors raise `SSException` rather than `UnboundLocalError` / `NotImplementedError`; a non-standard residue letter on the EV path raises an `SSException` naming the residue.
* **Uncapped chains: phi and psi columns were offset by one residue.** `get_angles('phi')` is undefined for the first residue of an uncapped chain and `get_angles('psi')` for the last, so the 2D method paired phi of residue *k+1* with psi of residue *k*. `SamplingQuality` now restricts every dihedral array to the residues that carry both angles and records them on the new `residue_indices` attribute. Capped chains are numerically unchanged.
* `verbose` flag gates the truncation print; docstrings corrected.

### Documentation
* `get_radius_of_gyration` and `get_gyration_tensor` state the actual convention (every atom weighted equally about the geometric centre; mdtraj's `compute_rg` is not mass-weighted, as the old docstring implied). `get_hydrodynamic_radius` documents the CA-based Nygaard inputs, the Kirkwood-Riseman prefactor and Pesce et al.'s harmonic averaging of the per-frame radii. `get_scaling_exponent` documents what `inter_residue_min` and `end_effect` actually drop; `calculate_all_CA_distances`, `get_dihedral_mutual_information`, the SASA family and `get_end_to_end_vs_rg_correlation` document their `weights` contract correctly.
* `sshdx` (module, functions and `docs/modules/hdx.rst`) describes the Best-Vendruscolo H-bond definition, the retained Wernet-Nilsson option and the size of the difference. `docs/modules/sssampling.rst` and `PrecomputedDihedralInterface` note that the precomputed EV reference resamples phi and psi independently (its joint distribution is the product of its marginals), so phi/psi correlation in the simulated ensemble contributes to the 2D distance; the rst also gains sections on bin width, relative-entropy smoothing, the summary figure's shared residue axis, and the seeded EV reference. `docs/modules/coper.rst` describes the two-stage feasibility check.
* `BMECustom` no longer claims its `theta` is on the same scale as `BME`'s: `BME` minimises `(m/2)·χ²_red + θ·KL` while `BMECustom` minimises `χ²_red + θ·KL`, so `BMECustom(theta=2θ/m)` reproduces `BME(theta=θ)`. `COPERResult.delta_S` documents that `delta_S = -KL` only holds for a uniform prior.
* `docs/usage/examples.rst` no longer labels the internal-scaling profile as a mean-square distance (`get_internal_scaling` returns the mean distance in Å; the RMS profile is `get_internal_scaling_RMS`) and describes the CA-based hydrodynamic radius; `docs/modules/ssprotein.rst` lists `length` as the method it is; the `ssnmr.rst` J-coupling example computes the ensemble mean through the `weights=` contract; `COPER.scan_chi2_limit`, `iCOPER.scan_chi2_limit`, the `predict` methods and `BMECustomResult.print_diagnostics` gained the `Parameters` sections they lacked; `ssdata.normalize_residue_name` and `ssutils.validate_stride` are listed in `utilities.rst`.
* `get_t`, `get_all_SASA`, `get_local_collapse`, `get_Q`, `get_angle_decay`, `get_sidechain_alignment_angle`, `get_interchain_distance_map`, `SSTrajectory.__init__`, `compute_random_coil_chemical_shifts` and `compute_J3_HN_HA` docstrings corrected to match behaviour.

## 2.0.5 (August 2026)

A correctness and documentation release from a follow-up package-wide review after 2.0.4: six bug fixes (one interpreter-crashing, several silently-wrong), a documentation audit pass, and expanded regression coverage. No new features and no API changes beyond stricter input validation.

### Bug fixes
* `sstrajectory.SSTrajectory(..., explicit_residue_checking=True)`: **Fixed a crash on topologies containing a chain with no protein residues** — the exact case the flag exists for (topologies carrying thousands of solvent molecules). A chain with zero valid residues contributed an *empty* atom list, and `atom_slice([])` built an empty topology whose first-residue lookup raised `IndexError`. Such chains are now skipped (with a debug message), matching what the default chain-detection path has always done.
* `ssprotein.get_RMSD` (and the shared region-selection helper behind `get_Q` and `get_D_vector`): **Fixed a fully out-of-range `region` producing garbage values and then segfaulting the interpreter.** An empty atom selection was passed straight to mdtraj's `rmsd()`, which returns uninitialized-memory values and crashes with SIGSEGV. A region that selects no atoms now raises a clear `SSException` naming the valid residue range. ## 2.0.4 (August 2026)

A feature release adding first-class SASA support for one-bead-per-residue coarse-grained models (Mpipi, HPS, KH and friends), computed against each model's own bead sizes rather than mdtraj's atomic van der Waals radii. Also fixes a correctness bug in the BBSEG secondary-structure assignment on uncapped chains, and corrects a batch of documented examples that did not match the API.

### Bug fixes
* `ssprotein.get_secondary_structure_BBSEG`: **Fixed the φ/ψ pair being drawn from two different residues on uncapped chains.** mdtraj returns one φ for every residue that has a preceding residue and one ψ for every residue that has a following one. On a capped chain the ACE/NME caps supply both, so the two arrays line up — but on an **uncapped** chain φ is undefined at the N-terminus and ψ at the C-terminus, so `phi[k]` and `psi[k]` describe two *adjacent* residues. The old code paired them positionally, so every BBSEG classification on an uncapped chain was made from a (φ, ψ) drawn from two separate residues. Separately, the returned `resid_list` was built from the full CA-bearing range and so came back one element longer than the classification arrays, meaning any caller zipping or plotting them together would misalign or raise. Each dihedral is now mapped back to its central residue via the atom indices mdtraj returns, and only residues with *both* angles defined are classified — so `resid_list` always matches the class arrays element-for-element. **This changes BBSEG output for uncapped chains** (which lose their two terminal residues, as they must); the committed reference values were rebuilt. **Capped chains are byte-identical** — verified by reproducing the old positional implementation and asserting agreement, and by a key-by-key diff showing the `ctl9_AA` / `gs6_AA` references did not move. A clear `SSException` is now raised when a selected region contains no residue with both angles defined (previously this produced a zero-width result).
* `ssprotein.get_local_heterogeneity`: fixed two coupled sliding-window off-by-one errors. The final valid window was omitted (and a full-chain window returned no data), while each included RMSD region contained `fragment_size + 1` residues because `get_RMSD` uses inclusive region bounds. The method now returns all `n_residues - fragment_size + 1` windows, each containing exactly `fragment_size` residues.
* `sstrajectory.SSTrajectory(..., protein_grouping=...)`: invalid unordered, duplicate, non-integer, empty, and overlapping residue groups now raise a clear `SSException`. These inputs were previously silently reordered or duplicated by MDTraj's topology selector, producing apparently valid but incorrectly grouped protein objects.

### Breaking changes
* **SASA on a one-bead-per-residue coarse-grained chain now requires a force field.** `get_all_SASA`, `get_regional_SASA` and `get_site_accessibility` raise an `SSException` on these chains unless the model whose bead sizes should be used is named — either per call (`forcefield='mpipi-gg'`) or once per protein (`protein.cg_forcefield = 'mpipi-gg'`). Previously these calls silently returned numbers computed with a 1.7 Å radius for *every* bead (see below), which is not a meaningful approximation for models whose bead diameters span 4.5–8.5 Å. There is deliberately no default force field: which model produced an ensemble is not something SOURSOP can infer, and guessing would silently change the answer. All-atom (and two-bead CA/CB) chains are unaffected and their output is byte-identical to 2.0.3; passing a `forcefield` to a chain that is *not* one-bead-per-residue also raises, rather than silently ignoring it.

### New features and improvements
* `ssprotein.get_all_SASA`: **Added a coarse-grained SASA path that uses per-residue bead sizes.** One-bead-per-residue models write every residue out as a single carbon `CA` atom, so mdtraj's Shrake–Rupley gave a glycine bead and a tryptophan bead the same 1.7 Å van der Waals radius. SOURSOP now builds a throwaway view of the topology in which each residue type carries its own bead radius, taken as `sigma/2` from the force field's own parameters (`sigma` is the model's diagonal (i,i) pair sigma, i.e. the bead diameter). Verified against the closed-form isolated-sphere area `4π(σ/2 + probe)²`. The caller's topology and coordinates are never modified, and the throwaway topology is built once per force field and reused across strides.
  * Note for anyone extending this: mdtraj's `change_radii` argument is keyed on `atom.element.symbol`, **not** on the atom name, and a name-keyed dictionary is *silently ignored* rather than rejected — the call succeeds and returns the default-radius answer. The coarse-grained path therefore varies each bead's element (using placeholder element symbols that cannot occur in a biomolecular trajectory), not its name.
* `ssdata`: **Added bundled bead-size tables for nine one-bead-per-residue force fields** under `soursop/data/cg/<slug>-sigma.csv` — `mpipi`, `mpipi-gg`, `mpipi-recharged`, `kh`, `hps-urry`, `hps-kr`, `fb-hps`, `cation-pi-1` and `cation-pi-2` — along with `ssdata.CG_FORCEFIELDS` (derived by listing the data directory, so a new table registers itself) and `ssdata.get_cg_bead_sigmas(forcefield, buried=False)`, which returns the residue-name → sigma mapping in Ångstroms. The `buried=True` variant exposes the sigmas of bead types 21–40 (the ~30% weaker copies these models define for residues sequestered inside a folded domain). The Mpipi variants additionally carry the RNA beads (`RPA`/`RPC`/`RPG`/`RPU`), so coarse-grained protein–RNA ensembles are supported.
* `ssprotein`: Added the `SSProtein.cg_forcefield` property (a validated, settable default force field, preserved across `reset_cache()`) and the `SSProtein.is_coarse_grained` property, which exposes the existing one-bead-per-residue detection. Note `is_coarse_grained` is distinct from `is_swan`: two-bead (CA/CB) models carry a CB for every non-glycine residue and take the all-atom code paths.
* `ssprotein.get_regional_SASA` / `get_site_accessibility`: both now accept and forward a `forcefield` argument.
* `ssprotein.get_all_SASA`: the memoisation key now includes the force field, so results computed with different bead sizes cannot collide. `mode='sidechain'`, `'backbone'` and `'all'` raise a clear `SSException` on one-bead-per-residue chains, where a single bead represents the whole residue and there is no backbone/sidechain decomposition to make; `mode='residue'` and `mode='atom'` are equivalent there and both work.

## 2.0.3 (July 2026)

A performance and correctness release: multi-model PDB files load roughly twice as fast, and a package-wide bug review fixed a number of correctness defects (several of which silently produced wrong numbers). A few fixes intentionally change numerical output — see **Bug fixes** below.

### Bug fixes
* `ssprotein.get_local_to_global_correlation`: **Fixed an off-by-one that excluded the C-terminal residue from every distance pair** (`range(start, end - seq_sep)` → `range(start, end - seq_sep + 1)`). The terminal residue never entered the local-vs-global correlation and the largest sequence separation contributed *zero* pairs, biasing every reported `mean_corr`/`std_corr`. **This changes the numerical output** of this function; the committed reference values were rebuilt.
* `ssprotein.calculate_all_CA_distances` (`mode='COM'`): **Fixed `stride` being silently ignored** — the COM branch called `get_inter_residue_COM_distance()` without forwarding `stride`, so `get_distance_map(mode='COM', stride=...)` (and `get_polymer_scaled_distance_map`) returned the unstrided result, and combining `mode='COM'` with `stride` *and* `weights` raised a `ValueError` (weights strided to a shorter length than the unstrided data). The `mode='CA'` path was always correct and is unchanged.
* `ssprotein.get_clusters`: **Fixed misuse of `scipy.cluster.hierarchy.ward`** — the full square RMSD matrix was passed directly, which scipy interprets as *observation vectors* (clustering on the Euclidean distance between RMSD-profile rows) rather than as a precomputed distance matrix. The matrix is now symmetrised and condensed with `scipy.spatial.distance.squareform` before clustering, so clustering is performed on the RMSD values as intended. **This changes cluster assignments.** A clear `SSException` is now raised when a stride leaves fewer than two frames to cluster (previously a cryptic scipy error).
* `ssprotein.get_secondary_structure_DSSP`: **Fixed per-residue H/E/C fractions not summing to 1** at residues DSSP cannot classify (mdtraj's `'NA'` code, emitted at chain termini / caps). `'NA'` now folds into coil (coil = neither helix nor strand), so the fractions form a complete partition summing to 1, as documented. **This changes the coil fraction at uncapped termini**; the committed reference values were rebuilt.
* `ssprotein.get_distance_map` (weighted): now returns `None` for the standard-deviation map when `weights` are supplied, as documented, instead of a NaN-filled array (assigning `None` into the float array silently produced NaNs).
* `ssprotein.get_angle_decay`: **Fixed a crash on single-frame trajectories** — a bare `np.squeeze` collapsed the frame axis when `n_frames == 1`, raising `AxisError`. Now squeezes only the length-1 atom axis.
* `ssprotein.get_asphericity`: added the divide-by-zero guard its siblings (`get_acylindricity`, `get_prolateness`) already have, so a degenerate (zero-trace) gyration tensor returns a finite value rather than `NaN` + a `RuntimeWarning`.
* `ssprotein.get_RMSD`: now accepts NumPy integer frame indices (e.g. from `np.arange`/`np.where`); `isinstance(frame2, int)` alone silently rejected `np.int64` and fell through to the compare-vs-all-frames branch.
* `ssprotein.get_Q` (`protein_average=False`): the returned residue-key list is now actually sorted (`np.sort(list)` returned a discarded array; replaced with `sorted(...)`).
* `ssnmr.compute_random_coil_chemical_shifts`: **Fixed a crash on phospho-Ser/phospho-Thr with `use_perdeuteration=True`** — the guard checked residue codes 22/25/28 but the phospho codes are `SEP=20, TPO=21, PTR=22`, so pSer/pThr slipped past and raised `IndexError` on the 20-element deuteration tables instead of the documented `SSException`.
* `ssnmr` (sequence parser): **Fixed an `IndexError` on out-of-range characters** — the bound `code > 35` was tested against a 26-element table, so characters mapping to codes 26–35 (e.g. lowercase `a`–`d`, `[ \ ] ^ _ \``) indexed out of range. Also fixed the amino-acid label list desyncing from the parsed sequence when a character is skipped, which mislabelled and dropped downstream residues.
* `ssdata`: **Fixed a merged residue name** — `"KM2KM3"` (a missing comma) is now `"KM2"` and `"KM3"`, so mono/di-methyl-lysine residues are recognised as valid protein residues rather than silently dropped during chain detection.
* `sstools.find_trajectory_files`: **Fixed replicate-directory matching** — `str.endswith(tuple_of_digit_strings)` matched trailing *characters* rather than the replicate *number*, so e.g. `rep15` was wrongly included for `num_replicates=5` (ends in `'5'`) while `rep7` was dropped. The trailing integer is now parsed and range-checked, so the correct set of replicates is gathered.
* `sspre.SSPRE`: clarified that `W_H` is the **angular** proton Larmor frequency (`omega_H = 2*pi*nu_H`, rad/s), which is what the Solomon–Bloembergen spectral-density term the code evaluates requires and what SOURSOP's shipped label-cloud calibration used. The docstring, example, and sanity-check range previously described `W_H` as the *linear* frequency (e.g. `600000000` for a 600 MHz magnet); following that guidance made the dispersive relaxation term a factor of `(2*pi)^2` too small (overestimating gamma by ~7% at `tau_c = 5` ns). **No numerical behaviour changed** — only the documentation and the validation-warning range/message.
* `sssampling.SamplingQuality`: **Fixed the default excluded-volume reference path crashing on uncapped chains** — the EV reference was gathered once per residue, but `get_angles('phi'/'psi')` returns one fewer angle than there are residues for an uncapped chain (phi undefined at the N-terminus, psi at the C-terminus), so the arrays failed to broadcast. The reference is now trimmed from the appropriate terminus to match.
* `sssampling.SamplingQuality`: **Fixed `truncate=True` crashing for `proteinID > 0`** — truncation rebuilds each trajectory from the single selected protein (now at index 0), but downstream code still indexed with the original `proteinID`, raising `IndexError`. `proteinID` is reset to 0 after truncation.
* `ssbme.BMECustom`: the default chi-squared path now uses its analytic gradient (`jac=True`) instead of a finite-difference gradient. The analytic `_default_cost_grad` was previously dead code and the docstring's claim of an analytic gradient was false; the finite-difference path cost O(N²·m) per optimizer step on the large ensembles the class targets. Custom cost functions still use finite differences. The optimum is unchanged (analytic gradient verified against finite differences to ~2e-9).
* `ssbme.BME`: dual Lagrange-multipliers are now initialised from a seeded local RNG rather than the global `np.random.normal`, so fits are reproducible run-to-run (the convex dual converges to the same optimum regardless, so results are otherwise unchanged).

### Performance
* `sstrajectory` (`__readTrajectory`): Multi-model PDB files — the standard output of idpconformergenerator, PED downloads, and NMR-style entries — now load **~2x faster** by parsing the file exactly once. When the trajectory file *is* the topology file and both are a PDB (i.e. `SSTrajectory(x.pdb, x.pdb)`), SOURSOP now calls `md.load_pdb()` directly rather than `md.load(..., top=...)`. The latter pre-parses the topology via mdtraj's `_parse_topology()` → `load_frame(top, 0)`, but mdtraj's PDB reader has no random access: it reads *every* model and then discards all but the first, so the whole file is parsed twice (once for the topology, once for the coordinates). On a 520 MB, 1000-model, 6.4M-atom tau ensemble this took loading from **58.9 s to 31.8 s**; the profile confirms the redundant pass (12,856,000 = exactly 2 × 6,428,000 `Atom.__init__` calls before the fix). The `pdblead=True` path no longer re-reads the same file a third time either. The returned trajectory is byte-for-byte identical (coordinates, topology, bonds, unit cell), and every other combination of inputs — including the common `xtc` + `pdb` case — takes the original code path unchanged. Note this removes the redundant pass only; the remaining cost is mdtraj's pure-Python parse of every `ATOM` record, so converting a large multi-model PDB once to a binary format (e.g. `.xtc`) remains far faster for repeated analysis.

## 2.0.2 (July 2026)

A feature release adding a coarse-grained spin-label model to the PRE calculation, and making it the default.

### Breaking changes
* **`sspre.SSPRE.generate_PRE_profile` now defaults to `use_label=True`.** By default the paramagnetic centre is now modelled as the coarse-grained spin-label cloud described below (calibrated against DEER-PREdict) rather than sitting directly on the `spin_label_atom` (the classic point-at-CB model). This produces different — and, against DEER-PREdict's explicit MTSL rotamer treatment, more accurate — I_para/I_dia and Γ₂ profiles than SOURSOP ≤ 2.0.1. **To reproduce results from earlier versions exactly, pass `use_label=False`.**

### New features and improvements
* `sspre.SSPRE.generate_PRE_profile`: Added a coarse-grained spin-label cloud model, controlled with `use_label` (now `True` by default; see above). Instead of placing the paramagnetic centre directly on the anchor atom, the unpaired electron is modelled as a deterministic cloud of `n_label_conformers` beads placed a fixed `label_distance` (default 7.0 Å, the mean CB→electron distance of the MTSSL 175K X1X2 rotamer library) from the anchor. When a CB distinct from CA is available the cloud is a cone (half-angle `label_cone_angle`, default 130°) about the CA→CB direction so it points away from the backbone; on coarse-grained CA-only chains it falls back to a full isotropic sphere, so the model works on both all-atom and one-bead-per-residue trajectories. Relaxation is averaged over the whole cloud *and* over frames, preserving the `r⁻⁶` non-linearity across both the conformer cloud and the ensemble. The cloud is generated on a Fibonacci lattice (no RNG), so profiles are fully reproducible.
* Steric exclusion of the label bead against the chain is always applied under `use_label=True` and can be either a `'hard'` cutoff (beads within `label_bead_radius` of any non-label CA are dropped) or a `'soft'` WCA-like repulsive wall (`w_i = exp(-Σ_j (label_bead_radius / d_ij)^label_wall_stiffness)`, reducing to the hard cutoff as the stiffness grows). The defaults (`label_steric='soft'`, `label_bead_radius=5.5` Å, `label_wall_stiffness=6.0`) were calibrated against DEER-PREdict's explicit MTSL rotamer PRE profiles across 85 disordered (trajectory) and ~2700 folded (AlphaFold) label sites; the soft wall was found to be marginally more accurate and about three times less sensitive to the bead radius than the hard cutoff, giving a single value that transfers between folded and disordered chains. This mimics the steric pruning DEER-PREdict applies to its rotamer library and stops the cloud being placed through the protein interior (critical for folded/compact chains).
* `sspre`: The residue iteration in `generate_PRE_profile` now uses the public `resid_with_CA` property rather than the private CA cache, so the PRE calculation works on coarse-grained one-bead-per-residue chains as well as all-atom trajectories. Atom coordinate lookups in the label-cloud path go through SOURSOP's memoised O(1) residue/atom lookup rather than a fresh `topology.select()` per call, keeping the calculation fast on large proteins.

* `sstrajectory.get_interchain_distance_map`: Hoisted the per-residue centre-of-mass computation out of the O(n1*n2) nested loop (chain-2 COMs were previously recomputed once per chain-1 residue). The non-periodic distance is now vectorised per row; the periodic path is preserved per-pair. Byte-for-byte identical output, with the redundant O(n1*n2) COM computations reduced to O(n1+n2).
* `sstrajectory.get_interchain_contact_map`: For the default `mode='atom'` (non-periodic), each residue's named-atom position is now computed once instead of via an O(n1*n2) loop of `get_interchain_distance` calls. Every other mode and the periodic path are unchanged. Output is byte-for-byte identical (verified against a pre-refactor baseline across CA/COM/atom and the untouched ca/closest-heavy/sidechain modes).
* `ssprotein` (`get_internal_scaling`, `get_scaling_exponent`, `get_local_to_global_correlation`): For `mode='CA'`, hoisted the per-residue CA centre-of-mass out of the O(n^2) sequence-separation loops via a new shared `__ca_position_cache` helper. Previously `get_inter_residue_atomic_distance` recomputed each residue's CA position from scratch on every pair (this path was *not* memoised, unlike the COM path). The helper reproduces the exact `__residue_atom_lookup -> atom_slice -> __get_subtrajectory -> 10*compute_center_of_mass` sequence, so results - including the seeded error-bootstrap / pair-resampling - are byte-for-byte identical, while the CA-position work drops from O(n^2) to O(n) (≈20-40x faster on the affected calls).
* `ssprotein.get_gyration_tensor` / `get_asphericity`: Vectorised the gyration-tensor computation across frames. The previous implementation looped over frames in Python, calling `mdtraj.compute_center_of_mass` once per frame and contracting one 3x3 second moment at a time; it now obtains the mass-weighted centre of mass for every frame in a single vectorised mdtraj call and contracts the second moment with a memory-batched `np.einsum` (the same mass-weighted COM and the same unweighted 1/N second-moment convention, so output is identical to floating-point precision). `get_asphericity` likewise replaces its per-frame `numpy.linalg.eig` loop with a single `numpy.linalg.eigvalsh` over the stacked tensors (valid because the gyration tensor is real-symmetric and the asphericity depends only on order-independent symmetric polynomials of the eigenvalues). Together this is ≈35x faster (≈230 -> ≈7,900 frames/s for a 109-residue chain). Removed the now-unused `from numpy import linalg as LA` import.
* `ssprotein`: Added two new gyration-tensor shape descriptors that reuse the (now cheap) vectorised eigendecomposition, both honouring the package-wide `weights=`/`etol` reweighting contract: `get_acylindricity` (the departure from cylindrical symmetry, `lambda_2 - lambda_1`, dimensionless `c/Rg^2` by default or raw Angstrom^2 with `normalized=False`) and `get_prolateness` (the gyration-tensor shape parameter `S = 27*prod(lambda_i - mean_lambda)/(sum lambda)^3` in `[-0.25, 2]`; `S > 0` prolate/rod-like, `S < 0` oblate/disc-like, `S = 0` spherical). Prolateness resolves a degeneracy in the asphericity: `get_asphericity` returns `delta* = 1 - 3*sum(lambda_i lambda_j)/(sum lambda)^2`, which is the relative shape anisotropy `kappa^2` and reports *how* anisotropic a conformation is but not whether it is rod- or disc-like. Validated against ideal shapes (rod `S=2`, disc `S=-0.25`, sphere `S=0`); `tests/test_ssproteins.py` gains a unit test covering the bounds and the weighted no-op contract.

### Consistent ensemble reweighting (frame `weights`)
* Every function that returns an ensemble-average value now accepts an optional per-frame `weights` vector, applied **consistently and deterministically** (closed-form weighted statistics; no stochastic resampling, fully reproducible, seed-independent). `weights=False` (the default) is an exact no-op, so all existing behaviour and the reference-observable suite are numerically unchanged.
* New shared validation/reduction helpers in `ssutils`: `validate_weights` (the single source of truth - raises `SSException` unless the vector has one finite entry per frame, every entry in `[0, 1]`, and sums to 1 within `etol`), plus `weighted_mean`, `weighted_rms`, `weighted_std` (reliability-weighted *population* estimator) and `weighted_corr`. `SSProtein.__check_weights` is now a thin wrapper around `validate_weights`.
* `stride` + `weights` is now correct: the weight vector is subsampled **and renormalised** to sum to 1 over the retained frames (previously it was silently left un-normalised).
* Converted the previously **stochastic** `numpy.random.choice` resampling in `get_internal_scaling`, `get_internal_scaling_RMS`, `get_scaling_exponent` (including its seeded error-bootstrap, now deterministically re-weighted per chunk) and `get_local_to_global_correlation` to deterministic weighted statistics, so weighting is uniform across the whole package. This also fixes a latent bug where `get_local_to_global_correlation` silently discarded user-supplied weights.
* Added the opt-in `weights` (and `etol`) parameter to the per-frame getters (`get_radius_of_gyration`, `get_hydrodynamic_radius`, `get_asphericity`, `get_end_to_end_distance`, `get_gyration_tensor`, `get_all_SASA`), to `SSTrajectory.get_overall_radius_of_gyration` / `_asphericity` / `_hydrodynamic_radius` and `get_interchain_distance_map`, and to the collapsing observables (`get_site_accessibility`, `get_regional_SASA`, `get_end_to_end_vs_rg_correlation`, `get_angle_decay`, `get_local_collapse`). When `weights` is supplied the frame axis is collapsed and the deterministic weighted statistic is returned.
* `get_internal_scaling(mean_vals=False, weights=...)` and `get_local_heterogeneity(weights=...)` raise a descriptive `SSException`: these describe a distribution / pairwise frame-vs-frame quantity for which a single per-frame probability vector is not well-defined (rather than silently returning a questionable number).
* `ssmutualinformation.calc_MI`: Fixed a latent bug (`if weights:` on a numpy array raised `ValueError: truth value ... ambiguous`) that made the weighted path of `get_dihedral_mutual_information` unusable for any real weight vector; now correctly guarded with `is not False`.
* Added the opt-in `weights` (+`etol`) parameter to five more methods so they collapse to a deterministic re-weighted ensemble value when supplied: `get_inter_residue_atomic_distance`, `get_inter_residue_COM_distance` and `get_t` (per-frame array -> scalar weighted mean; stride-aware), and `get_secondary_structure_DSSP` / `get_secondary_structure_BBSEG` **only when `return_per_frame=False`** (the per-residue fractional occupancies become frame-weighted; supplying `weights` with `return_per_frame=True` raises `SSException`, since a per-frame binary mask is not an ensemble average). Defaults (`weights=False`) are exact no-ops.
* `ssprotein.get_contact_map`: Removed the explicit "stride must be 1 if weights given" hard-block - `stride != 1` and `weights` now work together. The weight vector is validated by `ssutils.validate_weights` (one entry per trajectory frame, `len == n_frames`) and stride-subsampled/renormalised so it aligns 1:1 with the strided frames; the weighted map is the deterministic weighted mean of the per-frame contact maps. Default/unweighted behaviour unchanged.
* `ssprotein.get_Q`: Restructured into two explicit phases - (1) extract the reference frame and define the native contacts / reference distances `r0` from the **full** trajectory *first*, completely independent of stride/weights; (2) *then* resolve the analysis ensemble (apply the stride, validate the weights). This decouples `native_state_reference_frame` from the strided/re-weighted analysis and fixes a latent bug where the alignment (`superpose`) indexed the reference into the *strided* trajectory. `stride != 1` and `weights` can now be used together (previously hard-blocked). The weight contract is consistent with the rest of the package - one entry per trajectory frame (`len == n_frames`), validated by `ssutils.validate_weights` and stride-subsampled/renormalised to align 1:1 with the strided frames; the weighted `protein_average=False` breakdown is now averaged over exactly the same frame set as the unweighted path (the old code silently dropped the first frame only in the weighted branch). `protein_average=True` still (intentionally) refuses weights. Default/unweighted behaviour and the reference-observable suite are numerically unchanged.

### Reweighting against experimental data (BME / iBME and COPER / iCOPER)
* New `ssbme` module: maximum-entropy reweighting of an ensemble against experimental observables. `BME` implements Bayesian/MaxEnt reweighting (the regularised penalty `½χ² + θ·D_KL`, solved over the Lagrange-multiplier dual) with an L-curve `theta_scan` to choose `θ`, and `iBME` adds the iterative scale/offset refinement used for data with an unknown global scale (e.g. SAXS). Array-based API: `ExperimentalObservable` (with `equality`/`upper`/`lower` constraints), `BME(observables, calculated_values, initial_weights).fit(...)`, and a `BMEResult` carrying χ², the effective-frame fraction `φ`, diagnostics and a `predict()` for independent observables. Adapted from the reference Bottaro/Lindorff-Larsen BME (numpy/scipy only - no scikit-learn).
* A third variant, `BMECustom`, takes the experiment as a **raw vector + per-conformer matrix** (`(n_frames, m)`) plus an optional **custom goodness-of-fit function** `cost(experiment, calculated_values, weights) -> float` (lower = better). It is the penalty form of Bayesian Ensemble Refinement (minimise `cost(w) + θ·D_KL(w‖w0)` over the simplex via SciPy `L-BFGS-B` with a softmax reparameterisation); with no custom cost it reproduces BME's reduced χ² exactly. Useful for SAXS/PRE profiles where the cost wants to be log-scale χ², a covariance χ², Pearson correlation, etc.
* New `sscoper` module: Convex Optimization for Ensemble Reweighting (COPER; Leung et al., *JCTC* 2016). `COPER` solves the *primal* convex problem - maximise the ensemble entropy subject to a **hard** `χ² ≤ limit` constraint (no `θ`) - over the `N` frame weights via SciPy `trust-constr`, in the paper's two steps (χ²-minimisation/feasibility, then entropy maximisation). The simplex is enforced implicitly through a softmax reparameterisation (`w = softmax(z)`), so the optimiser handles only the few χ² constraints rather than `N` bound constraints plus a sum-to-one equality; this converges in tens of iterations and is hundreds to thousands of times faster than optimising the constrained weights directly (e.g. seconds rather than minutes for a 600-conformer SAXS profile), with an identical optimum. The entropy objective and χ² constraints further supply exact analytic Hessians as matrix-free `LinearOperator`s (diagonal entropy Hessian; rank-`≤ m` χ² Hessians), so no dense `N×N` matrix is formed and memory stays `O(N)` — a binding 100,000-conformer fit runs in tens of seconds where a dense Hessian would need ~80 GB. It explicitly reports **infeasibility** (when no reweighting can satisfy the data), supports **per-data-type** `χ²_α` constraints via the new `ExperimentalObservable.group` field, and reports the entropy reduction `ΔS` / mean free-energy change `⟨ΔG⟩/kT` / per-frame reweighting factors. `iCOPER` adds the iterative scale/offset refinement, and `chi2_limit_scan` is the error-scaling analogue of BME's L-curve.
* The reweighting primitives shared by both modules are factored into `ssutils` (`ExperimentalObservable`, `relative_entropy`, `weighted_linear_regression`, `find_optimal_theta`, `validate_reweighting_inputs`, `constraint_chi_squared`) and re-exported, so BME and COPER expose an identical user-facing interface. Weights produced by either plug directly into the package-wide `weights=` system above.

### Scalar (J) couplings in `ssnmr`
* `ssnmr` is no longer chemical-shifts-only: a new `compute_J3_HN_HA(protein, model="Bax2007", weights=False, ...)` computes the backbone ³J(HN, Hα) scalar coupling per frame per residue from the φ dihedral via the Karplus relation, using any of six literature parameterisations (`Bax2007`, `Bax1997`, `Ruterjans1999`, `Habeck`, `Vuister`, `Pardi`) exposed in `KARPLUS_HN_HA_COEFFICIENTS` + per-model uncertainties in `KARPLUS_HN_HA_UNCERTAINTIES`. Coefficients are ported from the [biceps package](https://github.com/vvoelz/biceps) (Voelz lab), itself adapted from MDTraj's `mdtraj/nmr/scalar_couplings.py`. A generic vectorised `karplus(angle, A, B, C, phi0=0.0)` is also exposed so any other Karplus-form relation (Bothner-By, Tvaroska, Aydin, ...) can be evaluated with the user's own coefficients. Output shape `(n_frames, n_phi)` is the natural input for `BME` / `BMECustom` / `COPER`; the function honours the package-wide `stride` + `weights` reweighting contract, and a `return_uncertainty=True` flag returns the model's forward-model σ in Hz alongside the J array.

### NOE distances in `ssnmr`
* New `compute_NOE_distances(protein, atom_pairs, stride=1)` returns the per-frame `(n_frames, n_pairs)` inter-atom distance matrix in Å for an arbitrary list of atom pairs (thin wrapper around `mdtraj.compute_distances` with the soursop unit + shape convention). A companion `noe_ensemble_average(distances, power=6, weights=False, ...)` collapses such an array via the NOE `(Σ w_i d_i⁻ᵖ)^(-1/p)` rule (defaults to `p=6`; `p=3` supported), honouring the package-wide `weights=` contract. The per-frame matrix (raised to the `-p` power) is BME/COPER-ready against an experimental NOE profile.

### HDX protection factors (new `sshdx` module)
* New `sshdx.compute_protection_factors(protein, beta_c=0.35, beta_h=2.0, beta_0=0.0, ...)` predicts per-residue ln(P) for backbone amide H/D exchange via the **Best–Vendruscolo** formula `lnP_i = β_c·N_c(i) + β_h·N_h(i) + β_0`. `compute_Nc` counts heavy atoms within 6.5 Å of the amide N of each residue (excluding `|i-j| ≤ 2`); `compute_Nh` counts backbone H-bonds the amide H of residue `i` forms (per-frame, via `mdtraj.wernet_nilsson`). All three helpers return `(residue_indices, (n_frames, n_residues) array)` so the per-residue mapping is explicit; the `(n_frames, n_residues)` shape is the natural input for BME / COPER reweighting against experimental HDX protection factors. Proline (no backbone H) and any residue lacking a recognisable backbone amide H are dropped; the package-wide `stride` + `weights` contract is honoured.

## Feb 2024 (0.2.5)
* Removed periodic correction in SSProtein functions. We could provide periodic boundary condition checks for some but not all functions, and ensuring this flag was possible everywhere is not feasable. With this in mind, we opted to make a design decision to remove the `periodic` flag from the small number of functions that had it, such that there's no risk of a user forgetting and analyzing two distinct properties for a trajectory that requires PBC fixing where one analysis used `periodic=True` whereas another did not have this option. Now, the user must ensure their protein trajectories are corrected ahead of time. Note that we also added support for periodic=True for some of the SSTrajectory analysis functions, because in cases where multiple chains are present reconstructing a non-PBC trajectory becomes much more difficult. As such, intermolecular analysis does provide intrinsic PBC correction, whereas intramolecular analysis does not.

## July 2022 (0.2.1)
For version 0.2.1 we introduce potentially breaking changes into how COM distances are reported.

**Details:** All center-of-mass (COM)-based functions now return distances (as before) and relative positions in x/y/z (this is new) in Angstroms, not nanometers. Previously, the various center-of-mass based functions that returned absolute positions (i.e. 3xn matrices of x/y/z vs frame number) returned them where x/y/z was in units nm. This is fine if you know this, but means if you manually calculate distance between two COM vectors you'd get a distance in nanometers and not Angstroms. This is an unhelpful and unexpected behavior given all other distances are in Angstroms, so we have made the decision to fully update to Angstroms even for vector positions. This DOES NOT break any code internal to soursop, but if you were using COM positions to manually calculate distances these may need to be recalculated.

## July 2021
CAMPARITraj is SOURSOP! For the final release we have re-named and re-branded CAMPARITraj as SOURSOP. This change in the name serves two important purposes.

Firstly, CAMPARITraj was borne out of a collection of scripts and code built to work with CAMPARI. However, it has evolved into a stand-alone package for the analysis of all-atom simulations of IDPs and IDRs, and importantly, much of the analysis it performs can also be done in CAMPARI. As such, we felt it was important to decoupled SOURSOP from CAMPARI, both to avoid the implication that CAMPARI cannot perform analyses itself, and to avoid a scenario in which it may appear that this package only works with CAMPARI simulations.

Secondly, there is a long, rich tradition of naming software tools after drinks in the Pappu lab (CAMPARI, ABSINTH, CIDER, LASSI etc.). As such SOURSOP is first-author Jared's Caribbean twist on this theme!

> **WARNING (historical):** We are currently and systematically updating all of the CAMPARITraj codebase to SOURSOP. As such, for now, we recommend using a previous version. Note the SOURSOP change breaks all backwards compatibility. Sorry about that.
